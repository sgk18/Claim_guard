# Slide 2: Problem — Expense Verification Is Still Too Manual

## SLIDE PURPOSE
Demonstrate the fundamental operational friction of traditional expense auditing without relying on fabricated statistics.

## SAY THIS (40s)
"The reality across most distributed organizations is that expense verification is still treated as an afterthought. Field employees collect crumpled paper bills or forward images over WhatsApp. Days or weeks later, a finance manager sits down to manually decipher faded ink, retype vendor names and totals into spreadsheets, and cross-reference dates. When thousands of receipts flow in, humans simply cannot detect whether the same fuel bill was submitted twice, whether a weekend dinner was tagged as client hospitality, or whether a printed tax number is even valid. The result is chronic administrative delay, costly duplicate payouts, and significant financial uncertainty."

## SIMPLE EXPLANATION
When people verify receipts by hand, they spend hours doing tedious data entry and comparing receipts one by one. It is easy for duplicate or incorrect claims to slip through unnoticed.

## TECHNICAL EXPLANATION
Manual workflows lack automated perceptual hashing for duplicate detection, cannot validate 15-character GSTIN checksums in real-time, and fail to track statistical spending anomalies against historical employee baselines.

## WHY IT MATTERS
Every unverified receipt represents potential policy leakage, lost Input Tax Credit (ITC), and wasted manager hours that could be spent on strategic financial planning.

## IMPORTANT CAVEAT
The problem is not that finance managers are careless; it's that humans are fundamentally ill-suited for high-volume, repetitive image comparisons and cryptographic checksum calculations under time pressure.

## TRANSITION
"This brings us to our core product thesis: what if every single receipt could be systematically screened before a manager ever spends a second reviewing it?"
